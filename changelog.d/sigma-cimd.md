- **Client ID Metadata Documents at `/authorize` and `/token` (Cloud MCP; mcp-doctor S5 (g) / S-46, task k174expfdyh954kzynmt9tyv9d8hxa1s).** An unregistered, URL-shaped `client_id` (Claude.ai's `https://claude.ai/oauth/mcp-oauth-client-metadata`) is resolved by fetching its document (draft-ietf-oauth-client-id-metadata-document-00; MCP authorization 2025-11-25) instead of answering `unknown-client`; the AS metadata now announces `client_id_metadata_document_supported: true`. SSRF bounds: https only on the default port, no userinfo, fragment, dot segments or IP-literal host in the `client_id`; DNS resolved once here, every answer must be public (private, loopback, link-local, CGNAT, multicast, reserved, cloud-metadata, and IPv6 forms embedding them are refused) and the socket is opened to that validated address, with the certificate checked against the hostname (no rebinding window); no redirect followed; body capped at 5 KB (declared and streamed); 3 s budget for DNS, connect and body; `Content-Type` must be JSON; at most 8 fetches in flight; cache bounded to 256 documents and a 1 h ceiling (5 min default, `no-store` honoured), errors and invalid documents never cached. The document must carry `client_id` equal to the URL, `redirect_uris` (exact match, no wildcard) and `token_endpoint_auth_method: "none"` (no `client_secret`); a refusal answers 400 `invalid_client` with `client-metadata: <reason>`. The document grants nothing: the token's rights come from the signed-in person and the organisation mapping, as for every client. Registered (DCR) clients keep their path and are never fetched. No Convex function or argument changes. Tests: `mcp-server/test/cimd-resolver.test.ts`, `mcp-server/test/cimd-authorize-token.test.ts`. Hardening after the dev-sentinel audit: `/authorize` can use at most 5 of the 8 fetch slots (3 are reserved for `/token`, so a person's refresh still resolves under a flood); one source address may start 10 fetches per minute, separately for `/authorize` and `/token` (cache hits are free; 429 `rate-limited` with `Retry-After`). Deviation from draft §4.4, stated: failed hosts are NOT negatively cached, because errors stay uncached as the draft requires; the starvation is bounded by the per-source limit plus the reserved slots, and a negative cache keyed on a URL the attacker chooses would not bound it. A failure that may pass (DNS, timeout, 5xx, busy, rate limit) answers 503 `temporarily_unavailable` so a client keeps its refresh token; only a wrong document is 400 `invalid_client`. The consent page names the redirect origin and flags the client as unverified; bidi, zero-width and format characters are stripped from `client_name`; the IPv4-mapped and SIIT ranges are refused, a trailing dot no longer hides `localhost.`, a `client_id` is capped at 512 characters, `/authorize` resolves the document once, the cache is a true LRU, and a refresh naming another `client_id` than the one it was issued to is `invalid_grant`. Revocation: `POST /admin/oauth/document-clients/deny` (`client_id`, `reason` of 20+ characters, `operator`, served by the existing `oauth:deleteClient` door: deleting a URL client lists it) lists a client in the new `oauth_denied_document_clients` table, revokes every access and refresh token it holds, and `/authorize`, `/token` and refresh refuse it before any fetch (a list that cannot be read answers 503, fail closed); `getAccessTokenByHash` also refuses any token row of a listed client (derived live, rights-derivation: the listing reaches tokens minted before it by construction). Lifting a listing is the operator deleting the row in the Convex dashboard (a new write door would raise the R-53 ratchet); revoked tokens stay revoked. Backend contract version 26 (Convex first, then the MCP). Tests: `convex/__tests__/documentClientDenial.test.ts`, `mcp-server/test/cimd-authorize-token.test.ts`.
