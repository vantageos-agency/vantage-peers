- **Client ID Metadata Documents at `/authorize` and `/token` (Cloud MCP; mcp-doctor S5 (g) / S-46, task k174expfdyh954kzynmt9tyv9d8hxa1s).** An unregistered, URL-shaped `client_id` (Claude.ai's `https://claude.ai/oauth/mcp-oauth-client-metadata`) is resolved by fetching its document (draft-ietf-oauth-client-id-metadata-document-00; MCP authorization 2025-11-25) instead of answering `unknown-client`; the AS metadata now announces `client_id_metadata_document_supported: true`. SSRF bounds: https only on the default port, no userinfo, fragment, dot segments or IP-literal host in the `client_id`; DNS resolved once here, every answer must be public (private, loopback, link-local, CGNAT, multicast, reserved, cloud-metadata, and IPv6 forms embedding them are refused) and the socket is opened to that validated address, with the certificate checked against the hostname (no rebinding window); no redirect followed; body capped at 5 KB (declared and streamed); 3 s budget for DNS, connect and body; `Content-Type` must be JSON; at most 8 fetches in flight; cache bounded to 256 documents and a 1 h ceiling (5 min default, `no-store` honoured), errors and invalid documents never cached. The document must carry `client_id` equal to the URL, `redirect_uris` (exact match, no wildcard) and `token_endpoint_auth_method: "none"` (no `client_secret`); a refusal answers 400 `invalid_client` with `client-metadata: <reason>`. The document grants nothing: the token's rights come from the signed-in person and the organisation mapping, as for every client. Registered (DCR) clients keep their path and are never fetched. No Convex function or argument changes. Tests: `mcp-server/test/cimd-resolver.test.ts`, `mcp-server/test/cimd-authorize-token.test.ts`.
